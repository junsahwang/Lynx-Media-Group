What is UGC? A plain-English guide

UGC stands for user-generated content — photos and videos that look like they were made by a real person, not a marketing department. This guide explains what UGC actually is, why short-form platforms reward it, and how brands and creators both profit from it.

The definition (and the twist)

Originally, UGC meant content customers posted on their own: reviews, unboxings, casual clips. The twist is that today most "UGC" is commissioned — brands pay creators to make videos in that authentic, handheld, talking-to-camera style, because it consistently outperforms polished ads on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts.

What UGC looks like

  • Talking-head reviews and recommendations
  • POV and storytime videos that mention a product naturally
  • Demos, tutorials, and "how I use it" walkthroughs
  • Day-in-the-life clips with the product woven in
  • Reaction and duet formats built on trends

Why platforms reward it

Short-form feeds rank videos by watch time, and people keep watching content that feels like a person, not a commercial. That's the whole mechanism: native-feeling video earns attention, attention earns distribution. For the deeper mechanics, read how TikTok and Reels reach really works.

UGC vs ads vs influencer marketing

Traditional ads rent placement. Influencer marketing rents an audience. UGC rents neither — it earns algorithmic reach on the strength of the content itself, which is why a brand with no following can still reach millions. We break down the comparison in our UGC vs influencer marketing FAQ.

Who's involved

Three parties: brands that want attention, creators who make the videos, and agencies like Lynx that connect the two and run the operation. If you're a business, start with UGC for brands. If you make content, read how to become a UGC creator.
