The algorithm, explained

How short-form reach really works

TikTok, Reels, and Shorts don't distribute videos because of who posted them — they test every single video on fresh audiences and promote the ones people actually watch. Once you understand that, the whole UGC playbook makes sense.

Every video gets an audition

Each new post is shown to a small test audience, mostly non-followers. Strong early watch time and engagement earn the next, bigger test — repeat until the video finds its ceiling. This is why follower count is a scoreboard, not a prerequisite.

Watch time is the currency

The metric platforms optimize is whether people keep watching. That makes the hook — the first two seconds — the highest-leverage part of any video, and it's why native-feeling UGC-style content beats polished ads in the feed.

Proof: reach without an audience

One Lynx client generated 4.29 million views from 145 posts on an account with 2,672 followers — roughly 1,600 views per follower. The content carried the reach; the account size was irrelevant. Full numbers in our case studies.

Reach is probabilistic — so volume wins

Nobody can predict which video hits; a minority of posts drive the majority of views. The rational strategy is consistent volume plus weekly iteration on what the data says — which is exactly how our Medceptor campaign turned 1,200 posts into 4.1 million views in 30 days.

One video, three platforms

The same vertical video publishes to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts — three algorithmic lotteries for the price of one upload.
